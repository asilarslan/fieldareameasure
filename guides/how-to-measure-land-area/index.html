How to measure land area

You can find the area of almost any piece of land without special equipment. Here are four methods, from quickest to most precise.

Updated: 2026-11-24

  1. Mark the corners on a satellite mapThe fastest way for fields and plots you can see from above. Find the land in a measuring app, tap each corner and read the area and perimeter. Accuracy depends on the image and how carefully you place the corners — usually within a few percent.
  2. Walk the boundary with GPSUseful under trees or where the edges aren't visible from above. Phone GPS is typically accurate to a few metres, which matters more on small plots.
  3. Tape measure and trianglesFor small gardens: measure the sides, split the shape into rectangles and triangles, and add them up (see our guide to irregular plots).
  4. Official surveyFor legal boundaries, sales and building permits, only a licensed surveyor's measurement counts.

Tips for better results

  • Zoom in before placing corners; place them where the boundary actually is (fence, hedge, ditch), not on shadows.
  • Add extra points on curved edges.
  • Compare your result with the area on the deed or land registry; small differences are normal.

FAQ

Can I measure land area with my phone?

Yes. Mark the corners on a satellite map in an app, or walk the boundary with GPS.

Why is my measurement different from the deed?

Deeds may use older surveys, different boundaries or rounding. For a legal figure, ask a surveyor.
